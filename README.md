# Conquest: souls-like character controller

A Unity 6 project with a third-person melee controller in the style of Dark Souls, plus a
low-poly knight. The knight's model, rig and 26 animations are all generated by a Blender script.

## Quick start

1. Open the `Conquest` folder in Unity Hub (**Add → Add project from disk**) with Unity 6 (6000.0 LTS or newer).
   Unity creates `ProjectSettings/` on first open and installs the Input System package from `Packages/manifest.json`.
   If Unity asks to enable the new input backends, click **Yes**. Unity then restarts.
2. In the menu bar choose **Conquest → Build Knight Sandbox**. This builds:
   - `Art/Characters/Knight/Animation/Knight.controller`: the locomotion blend tree, the action states and an upper-body guard layer
   - `Data/Moveset/Knight/*.asset`: the light combo (3 hits) and the heavy attack
   - `Prefabs/Characters/Player_Knight.prefab` and `Enemy_Knight.prefab`
   - `Prefabs/Environment/...`: the desert kit prefabs and the boss arena (see below)
   - `Scenes/Sandbox.unity`: the desert boss arena with three sparring knights and a HUD
3. Open `Assets/_Project/Scenes/Sandbox.unity` and press Play.

## Cameras

The sandbox starts in an **isometric** view built with Cinemachine. It uses an orthographic
camera at 35° pitch and 45° yaw that follows the knight. When you lock on, it blends to a second
camera that keeps the knight and the target in frame together. In this view, lock-on picks targets in
front of the knight rather than in front of the camera. Press **V** to switch to the orbiting
third-person camera and back. The Cinemachine parts live in their own assemblies
(`CameraSystem/Cinemachine`, `Editor/Cinemachine`), and those only compile when the Cinemachine
package is installed. You can tune angle, distance and zoom on the `Isometric Camera Rig` object.

## Desert boss arena and art direction

The sandbox now takes place in the boss arena from the concept art: a round fighting floor with an
inlaid ring, a low ring wall, tiered stands, banner pillars with fire bowls, red awnings, the temple gate to the
north and bridges over the canyon to the east, west and south. You enter from the south path; the three
sparring knights wait in the middle.

**Look.** Everything uses `Conquest/Toon Flat` (Art/Shaders): flat colour per surface, a hard light/shadow
split tinted purple, painted grit noise and wind sway for cloth. A full-screen pass (`Conquest Outline`, added to
every URP renderer as a Full Screen Pass Renderer Feature) draws the ink lines from depth and normals. The
`Desert_Atmosphere` prefab holds the sun, the gradient sky and the warm haze. The knights' materials are
converted to the same shader when they are imported.

**Building your own levels.** Every part of the arena is its own prefab under
`Prefabs/Environment/DesertKit/<Category>` (Floor, Walls, Architecture, Props, Cloth, Tech, Nature, Backdrop), with
its collider, fire light and shared palette materials already set up. Drop `Desert_Atmosphere` into a scene and
start placing pieces. Ring pieces (`Floor_Arena_Segment`, `Floor_Walkway_Segment`, `Wall_Arena_Segment`,
`Stands_Segment`) all pivot on the arena centre, so put them at one point and rotate them in 45° or 15° steps;
leave wall segments out to make openings. Add `SpawnPoint` components (Player or Enemy) and the sandbox builder spawns
the knights on them. `Arena_DesertBoss` in `Prefabs/Environment/Levels` is just one arrangement of the kit, with
invisible cliff walls on the Ignore Raycast layer. Recolour the whole kit in `Tools/Blender/arena/palette.py` (the `Kit_*` materials in
`Art/Environment/Materials` follow it).

**Blender.** The kit is generated like the knight:

```
blender --background --python Tools/Blender/build_arena_kit.py -- \
    --out Assets/_Project/Art/Environment/DesertKit [--preview <folder>] [--only Pillar_Banner,Brazier_Bowl]
```

- `arena/palette.py`: the colours from the concept, with grit, sway and glow per material
- `arena/pieces_*.py`: one function per kit piece (architecture, props, tech, nature). Add a function with the
  `@piece(...)` decorator and it becomes a new FBX and prefab.
- `arena/layout.py`: the boss arena as a list of placed pieces, spawns and cliff walls (exported to
  `Layouts/BossArena.json`)
- `DesertKit.blend` (in `DesertKit/Source~`, hidden from Unity) has every piece on a grid plus the assembled arena.

After re-running the script, choose **Conquest → Rebuild Desert Kit Prefabs** (or **Build Knight Sandbox** to also
rebuild the scene). The `Kit_*` materials are re-synced from `palette.py` on every rebuild; duplicate one if you want a custom colour for a level.

## Combat feel

Hits are meant to land hard. All of it is data, so you can tune it while playing:

- **Hit-stop:** both fighters freeze for a few frames when a blow connects. The defender's model shudders along the blow. Heavier blows freeze longer. The attacker's freeze is capped (`maxAttackerHitStop` on the profile) so landing a hit never stalls your next input.
- **Flow:** a press made during a swing waits for the combo window instead of expiring, and the buffer stands still during hit-stop. A press during a weapon's cooldown swings as soon as it ends. Moving the stick or raising the guard breaks out of the end of an attack (`moveCancelFrom`) or a roll. Pressing light attack out of a roll or backstep does the moveset's roll attack (`rollAttack` on the first attack of the chain), and either attack in the air does a plunging jump attack (`jumpAttack`).
- **Camera:** shake, a jolt along the blow and a small zoom punch. The orbit camera and the isometric Cinemachine cameras read the same shared signal (`CameraShake`, plus `CinemachineShakeExtension` on each Cinemachine camera).
- **Weight:** each `AttackData` has a `weight` (1 = light, 2 = heavy). Weight scales knockback, hit-stop, shake and blood. Blows at or above `heavyHitWeight` stagger instead of flinch. The attacks hold a wind-up, strike over three frames, overshoot, and then have a long recovery. The body shifts back during the wind-up and lunges into the strike.
- **Blood:** in a flat Sable style. Hits spray droplets along the blow and the swing, splats land where the droplets fall, and a pool spreads under the dead (`BloodSpray`, `SplatDecals`, shader `Conquest/Flat Splat`).
- **Everything else:** a white hit flash, sparks off shields, a bright flash and short slow motion on parries and kills, a flat swoosh trail behind the blade (`WeaponTrail`), whooshes, flesh hits and shield clangs, and a red screen-edge flash when you are hurt.

What each kind of hit (damaged, blocked, guard break, parried, killed) does is set in one asset,
`Data/Feedback/HitFeedback.asset`. The sounds are placeholders synthesised by
`Tools/Audio/make_combat_sfx.py`. To use real sounds, replace the clips in that asset and on the `SwingSound` component.
**Conquest → Capture Combat Effects** renders a still of the effects to `Captures/combat_fx.png`.

## Weapons

`WeaponLoadout` (on each character) lists the weapons the model carries: model, blade points and hit radius.
Equipping one shows its model, hides the rest and hands its blade to the hit detector and the swing trail.
The player starts with the **Ultra Greatsword** and the enemy with the **Longsword**. Change `equipped` on the
component, or call `Equip(...)` from code, to switch.

A weapon can also bring its own **animation set** and **moveset**. The greatsword uses
`Knight_TwoHanded.overrideController`, which swaps every clip that has a two-handed variant (`Idle_2H`, `Run_F_2H`,
`Guard_Idle_2H`, `Attack_1_2H`, ...) into the same states, plus the greatsword attacks in `Data/Moveset/Greatsword`.
Two-handed, the knight rests the flat of the blade on his right shoulder, hilt in his right fist and the left arm free,
like Elden Ring's greatsword idle; the left fist joins the hilt for swings and the guard. Rolls, parry, stagger, guard
break and death are shared. Weapons without a set (the longsword) use the one-handed clips and `Data/Moveset/Knight`.
To add a set for another weapon, add `_<suffix>` clips in `animations.py` and build an override with
`AnimatorControllerBuilder.BuildOverride`.

## How it plays

- **Free camera:** the knight turns toward where you move and plays the forward walk, run and sprint animations.
- **Locked on:** the knight keeps facing the target and the camera frames both of you. The 2D blend tree picks walk or run forward, back, left or right from the move direction relative to the target. Rolls go in the stick direction with a matching directional roll. With no stick input, a dodge is a backstep.
- **Attacks:** with the greatsword, a two-handed moveset modelled on Elden Ring's ultra greatswords: pressing attack during an attack's combo window chains a cleave from the right shoulder, a return sweep and an overhead slam that buries the blade. Each greatsword swing lifts the lead foot in the wind-up and stomps a step forward (about 0.7–0.95 m) with the strike, then the rear foot catches up in the recovery; the distances are `Step`s in `AttackAssetFactory`. With the longsword, quick one-handed slashes and an overhead chop. Early presses are buffered for 0.35 s. You can cancel an attack's recovery with a dodge. The attacker can still turn toward the target during wind-up.
- **Charged heavy:** the heavy attack hauls the blade up behind the head. Keep holding the heavy button to hold that pose for up to 1.2 s; the longer you hold, the harder it hits (up to 1.6× damage and a heavier blow). Release to slam, or roll out of the charge. Any `AttackData` can be made chargeable with its Charge settings.
- **Stamina:** an action can start whenever any stamina is left, and its full cost is then taken. Regeneration pauses briefly after each use and slows while blocking.
- **Sprint:** 7.2 m/s against a 4.2 m/s run, draining 14 stamina per second. When the bar empties he drops to a run and can't sprint again until you let go of sprint.
- **Block:** works while walking because the guard pose lives on an upper-body animator layer. Blocked hits cost stamina. If stamina runs out the guard breaks and the knight staggers.
- **Greatsword heft:** the player's greatsword swings play with a tempo curve (`AttackData.tempo`): the wind-up drags and hangs for a beat at the top, the strike snaps through faster than the clip, and the recovery drags. The blows are heavier (weight 1.6 to 2.6: more hit-stop, shake and knockback), the aim commits earlier, and each swing jolts the camera (`SwingShake`, player only). After a greatsword swing the next one can't start straight away: a combo only continues once the blade has settled, and when a swing ends there is a 0.35 s cooldown (`AttackData.cooldown`) during which attack presses are ignored. The enemy's longsword is unchanged.
- **Jump:** E / A (Cross) jumps about 1.1 m high (`JumpState`, `JumpSettings`). The jump keeps your run-up speed, lets the stick steer a little in the air and costs 10 stamina. You can only jump from the ground. A button pressed in the air (attack, roll) fires on landing.
- **Enemy AI:** the enemy (`MeleeAIBrain`) attacks with single swings, never a combo (`maxComboLength` 1), and never blocks (`blockChance` 0). Raise either on the enemy prefab to bring them back.
- **Enemy health bars:** every enemy has a health bar floating over its head (`OverheadBar` on a world-space canvas with a `ResourceBar`). It faces the camera, keeps the same size on screen with perspective or orthographic cameras, shows a gold trail for recent damage, fades out very far from a perspective camera (50–60 m) and disappears shortly after the enemy dies.
- **Energy shield:** while guarding, a faceted cyan bubble (`EnergyShield`, `Art/Shaders/EnergyShield.shader`) surrounds the character. It ripples out from each blocked hit, flickers below 25% stamina and shatters on a guard break. Blocked hits play synthesised energy zaps (`Shield_Hit_*`, `Shield_Break`, `Shield_Up` from `Tools/Audio/make_combat_sfx.py`) and spark cyan. Any character with a `CombatReceiver` can use it.
- **Gun parry:** the player parries with a pistol in the left hand, as in Bloodborne. Parry draws it, fires on frame 5 of the 20-frame `Shoot` clip and holsters it again; a dodge can cancel the recovery. The shot (`ParryGun`, a 12 m sphere cast at chest height) parries an enemy that is winding up or swinging: from 0.35 s before its hit window opens (`AttackData.parryLead`) until the window closes. A parried enemy is staggered for 1.6 s, with the usual parry slow motion and ring. A shot at any other time only chips 4 health, too light to interrupt (below `ReactionSettings.flinchWeight`). `GunFeedback` adds the muzzle flash, tracer and the synthesised `Gun_Shot`. Otherwise the pistol sits holstered at the small of the back.
- **Parry (no gun):** characters without a `ParryGun`, like the enemy, keep the blade parry: a hit that lands in the parry window (frames 2–10) does no damage and staggers the attacker for 1.6 s.
- **Dodge:** i-frames cover most of the roll.
- **Nano-injector (heal):** R / X (Square) heals the player like an Estus flask. The knight pulls a glowing blue cartridge from his belt, slams it into the port on his right gauntlet (frame 20 of the 48-frame `Heal` / `Heal_2H` clip) and the nanites restore 45% of max health over 0.9 s, with a blue flash, sparks, rising motes and synthesised sounds (`Injector_*`, `Nanite_Repair`). He is committed for the whole 1.6 s: no moving, turning or cancelling. A hit before the slam interrupts it and keeps the charge; after the slam the repair carries on. It costs no stamina. He carries 4 cartridges (`NanoInjector`), shown as blue pips under the stamina bar (`ChargeCounter`); with none left the button only clicks. Charges (and health) refill at a **checkpoint** (`Checkpoint`, a nanite station next to the spawn: walk into it) and when he respawns there 4 s after dying (`PlayerRespawn`). Enemies are not reset on respawn. **Conquest → Install Nano-Injector** (or `ConquestInjectorInstall.pending`) adds all of this to an existing project in place; **Conquest → Test Nano-Injector** (or `ConquestInjectorTest.pending`, optionally containing a scene path) plays it through and writes `Captures/Injector`.

## Architecture

Each folder under `Assets/_Project/Scripts` is its own assembly (`.asmdef`). Dependencies only point
downward, so you can reuse any module in another project or for another character.

```
Core          state machine, ResourcePool, InputBuffer, TimeWindow, ICharacterInput, ILifeState
├─ Stats        Health, Stamina                      (MonoBehaviours around ResourcePool)
├─ Movement     CharacterMotor                       (CharacterController wrapper: velocity, gravity, turning)
├─ Animation    CharacterAnimator, AnimatorParameters (typed facade, the only place with animator strings)
├─ Targeting    Targetable, TargetSelector, LockOnController
│  └─ CameraSystem  ThirdPersonCamera, ILookInput
│     └─ Cinemachine  IsometricCameraRig, CameraModeSwitcher (only with the Cinemachine package)
├─ Combat       DamageInfo, CombatReceiver (block/parry/i-frame rules), Hurtbox, MeleeHitDetector, ParryGun, AttackData
│  └─ Feedback   HitFeedback + profile, HitFlash, BloodSpray, SplatDecals, WeaponTrail, VfxPool, TimeDilation, audio
├─ Character    ActionCharacter + states (Locomotion, Attack, Dodge, Parry, Shoot, Reaction, Dead)
├─ Player       PlayerInputReader (Input System), CursorLock
├─ AI           MeleeAIBrain
├─ UI           ResourceBar, LockOnReticle
├─ Environment  SpawnPoint, AtmosphereSettings, FlameFlicker, Hover (no dependencies)
└─ Editor       model import rules, animator/prefab/scene builders, desert kit builder, menu
```

Key ideas:

- **One character, many drivers.** `ActionCharacter` only reads `ICharacterInput` (intent) and
  `ITargetProvider` (focus). `PlayerInputReader` and `MeleeAIBrain` both implement these interfaces,
  so the enemy is the same prefab setup as the player with a different brain.
- **Rules live in one place.** States only set stance flags (`IsInvulnerable`, `IsGuarding`,
  `IsParrying`). `CombatReceiver` decides whether a hit is evaded, blocked, parried, breaks the guard or does damage.
- **Data-driven timing.** Attacks are `AttackData` assets with normalized windows (active, combo,
  dodge-cancel, tracking) and a lunge curve. No animation events are needed, so you can rebalance attacks without
  touching code or clips.
- **No weapon colliders.** `MeleeHitDetector` sweeps spheres between blade points every frame, so fast
  swings can't pass through targets. Each target is hit once per swing, and allies are ignored by `Faction`.

### Adding things

- **New attack:** create it from **Create → Conquest → Combat → Attack**, set its `animationState` to a state in the
  controller, and link it from another attack's `next` field or assign it as the light or heavy attack.
- **New enemy type:** the **Bulwark** (`Enemy_Bulwark.prefab`) is the parent of every enemy. Build each new enemy as a
  Prefab Variant of it with `CharacterPrefabBuilder.BuildEnemyVariant(heavyEnemy, setup, path, customize)` in
  `ConquestSetupMenu.Build`: set `setup.Model` / `setup.Controller` to swap the model and animator (references into the
  old model are re-pointed by name), and use `customize` to change the brain or stats. The Sentinel (`Enemy_Knight`)
  and the Warden (`Enemy_Warden`, see `MakeGunner`) are built this way. For a new model, start from the Bulwark's
  Blender parts in `Tools/Blender/knight/brute_model.py` and keep the knight's rig (bone and socket names).
- **Swap input bindings:** they are defined in `PlayerInputReader.CreateActions()`. You can also replace the component with your own
  `ICharacterInput`.

## Blender workflow

The knight is generated, not hand-modelled, so edit the Python modules and re-run the build:

```
blender --background --python Tools/Blender/build_knight.py -- \
    --out Assets/_Project/Art/Characters/Knight/Knight.fbx \
    --blend Assets/_Project/Art/Characters/Knight/Knight.blend \
    [--preview <folder>]        # renders 4 frames of every clip
```

- `--model tech` (the default) builds the tech-knight from `knight/tech_model.py`: tall helm with a cyan visor, red cowl and
  tattered cloak, surcoat, tech backpack, forearm bracer (in place of the shield) and a dark sword with a glowing edge.
  `--model classic` builds the original armoured knight from `knight/model.py`. Both use the same rig and clips.
  Soft parts (cloak, coat) are skinned across several bones so they follow the hips and legs.
- `--turnaround <folder>` renders front, side, back and 3/4 views of the idle pose (`knight/turnaround.py`).
- In Unity, **Conquest → Capture Character Shots** (or a `ConquestCharacterCapture.pending` file in the project root) renders
  the player prefab with the game shaders into `Captures/Character`: a turnaround plus a strip of action poses.
- **Conquest → Test Sprint** and **Conquest → Test Block** (or `ConquestSprintTest.pending` / `ConquestBlockTest.pending`; the sprint test also jumps)
  play the open scene with virtual keys and log speed, stamina and guard state to `Captures/`; the block test also
  renders close-ups of the energy shield into `Captures/Block`.
- **Conquest → Test Gun Parry** (or `ConquestGunParryTest.pending`) stands the enemy in front of the player, shoots it
  once while it idles (chip only) and once as it swings (parry), and logs and renders both into `Captures/GunParry`.
  Character materials whose name ends in `Glow` are made emissive on import.
- `knight/rig.py`: bones and sockets (`Blade_Base`, `Blade_Mid`, `Blade_Tip`, `LockPoint`), plus the two-handed grip:
  IK constraints put both wrists on the `SwordCtrl` and `Grip.L` control bones. A clip's `grip` keys blend between
  free FK arms (0) and both hands on the hilt (1); `left` overrides the left hand alone. The grip is baked into the FBX, so Unity only sees bone rotations.
- `knight/model.py`: the low-poly armour, sword and heater shield, rigidly bound to bones
- `knight/weapons.py`: the tech-knight's weapons, each a separate mesh on the `Weapon.R` bone with its own blade
  points (`Greatsword_0..3`, `Longsword_0..2`). The ultra greatsword is a 1.55 m slab with a two-hand grip and cyan edges;
  the longsword is the original blade.
- `knight/posing.py`: posing in armature axes (`X+` = lean forward, `Z+` = turn left) with absolute overrides.
  In the attacks, `grip(wrist, pitch, roll, yaw)` places the hands and aims the blade.
- `knight/animations.py`: all clips as key poses. If you change an action's length, also change its
  `AttackData.duration` or the matching dodge, parry or reaction setting in `ActionCharacter`.

`Knight.blend` is saved next to the FBX so you can also open it and polish clips by hand.
If you do, export with the settings in `knight/export.py`. Unity re-imports the FBX automatically, and the import rules
(Generic rig, axis fix, clip names, looping) are applied by `CharacterModelPostprocessor`. To pick up the changes, run
**Conquest → Rebuild Knight Animator Only**.

## Notes and next steps

- Everything compiles against the Unity API. It hasn't been play-tested in the Editor yet, so expect some
  tuning of speeds, timings and camera feel.
- The animations are blocked-out key poses rather than motion capture. They read clearly but are stiff.
  Mixamo or hand-keyed clips can replace them without code changes, as long as the state names stay the same.
- Ideas: poise/hyper-armour, riposte after a parry, jump attacks, root-motion attacks,
  Cinemachine camera, and saving with bonfires.
